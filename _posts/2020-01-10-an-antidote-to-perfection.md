With the the new year being in the starting gates, now might be the perfect time to re-think your entire life and become a superhuman. Just kidding. This won't be another article on how to "reinvent yourself in 2020". I mean, well, it will be to an extent. But the topic shouldn't be related to the new year. And to be honest, this exact mindset of setting sky-high goals for the upcoming year and "how to become insanely productive get ahead in life" whenever we have five minutes of leisure time, kills people's sanity.

But what am I even talking about? Perfection and productivity. They're really helpful in a lot of aspects but toxic if used wrongly. I can only tell from my life, but ever since I can think, I'm all about giving everything I have – so much that it has become part of my personality. 100 percent, and even that's never enough. And let's be honest, the mindset has brought me far. My grades at school and university were and are always great and my resume is impressively long for my age. But while growing as a person, I increasingly ask myself: What is all the effort for? During stressful times, my life always turns into a weird kind of robot-movie where I do indeed function but simply can't enjoy anything outside work. Whenever this happens, my creativity plummets and I just generally feel exhausted. Nonetheless, I keep doing. Because you gotta perform as well as you possibly can, right? You know what? Fuck this.

## Sit back 
If I can urge anyone to do one thing in 2020, then it's this: Chill. The fuck. Out. Especially among my generation I often observe a huge pressure we all put on ourselves. Everyone and everything needs to be better and greater. Competition becomes a lifestyle. Do you really need to score a perfect grade in that exam? And do you really need to finish your assignments at work a week earlier? A few days later, most of it won't even matter anymore. A constant do-more mindset that doesn't include time to recharge will only slowly push you towards burnout while killing your creativity and happiness. At least that's what I've experienced on myself and among peers. 

More doesn't always equal better – especially not more pressure.   Seriously. It's okay to not be perfect. We don't need to drive ourselves insane for things that don't even matter. Even more, isn't beauty defined by imperfection? Not being able to deal with imperfect results, days and relationships is just as hurtful as not being able to deal with them at all. You can't do it all at once. Nobody can. Still, we're told to do exactly that. Surely, there are exceptions – but from what I've experienced, the narrative among academics currently sounds something like: "You want to live a happy and fulfilled life? Then you better put everything into outperforming your competition. ... And in you leisure time, you can work on your side-hustle." Performance equals success equals happiness. Rubbish. Success won't make anyone happy. Meaning and impact will.

## Hustle culture on social media
Though, why is it that the world has become so competitive? Sure, competition is natural in our capitalist society – but today, it's become more extreme than ever. One reason, why we experience such a large pressure might be the toxic images of work and success portrayed social media. We all know that social media is toxic as whole – but nothing makes me more angry when I observe the "hustle culture" that has developed online. I've been digging into the self-development- and work-bubble on social media for a while now. And for a long time, things were simple. 

In 2014, the self-development community was situated mainly on YouTube and Reddit and the content was primarily about how to take control of your life and form good work habits. Over the recent years however, drastic changes happened. Successful entrepreneurs like Gary Vaynerchuck or Casey Neistat realized the potential of these platforms and started to pump out a ton of high-quality content. By doing so, they started a new gold-rush for success. Thousands of people followed their lead and formed a new hustle culture with a narrative that idealises extremes. Working 15 hours a day becomes the new heroin. Your work defines you as person. I seriously start doubting humanity when an audience starts cheering to sentences like "You're gonna die, so you better work harder" or "I spent less time off work during my 20s than you spent last weekend". Yes, you gonna die. And that could happen either in 50 years or tomorrow. And I'm not willing to put myself in a position, where everything I leave on this planet is a pile of work.

## Let's flip the coin
Recently, this type of hustle-content has spread around the internet like a wildfire. And honestly, there's nothing wrong with working hard. Working hard is great. Everybody should do it. However, it's not the only aspect of life. Social relationships and hobbies are just as important. They might even  offer a better perspective of leave a lasting impact on this planet. Humanity is defined by people, not revenues. Remember that.

Very few persons can pull off a hustle-lifestyle – and when they do, they have to deal with difficulties in other areas of life. Casey Neistat nearly broke his marriage by producing daily vlogs. Some of my favourite YouTubers who jumped into the hustle-life suddenly disappeared and came back with a video on burnout a few months later. And very few of the extremely hard-working people (to an extent that it's definitely unhealthy) I've met personally seemed to have a stable mental state or social environment. There's always another side to every story.

In this regard, what the hell even happened to hobbies? Nobody seems to have hobbies anymore aside from travelling and watching Netflix from time to time. Everything we do has to be productive. Everything has become a hustle. If I like designing computer games, why can't I just day for the sake of it? Not everything has to bring in money. It's great when you work in a field you're passionate about but work can't replace off-time.

## Practical tips
The point I'm trying to make is: Don't give in to the external pressure. Don't compare. You do you. Other people's achievements don't matter. In the age of social media, it feels like we have to re-learn to focus on ourselves. Listen to your body and emotions first. Your mental and physical health and your relationships are more valuable than any of your work could ever become.

In terms of work-ethic, don't strive for perfection. Strive for progress. Be just a little bit better than yesterday. Eventually, this will lead your to goal. Maybe, it might take you longer than someone else – but that's okay. You don't know about the other person's starting point. 

Pressure only works paired with relaxation. If you expand a spring and never let it jump back it will eventually break. And a car that never stops to re-fuel won't go far. The same goes for us as humans.
