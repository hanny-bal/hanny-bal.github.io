Just when you think your life is on track, the unexpected happens. A telltale about expectations, goals and happiness.

 I remember playing this game called “Lego Racers” as a child. On one race-track, the route split up into two paths. One of the paths seemed to be a shortcut and led me through a ancient pyramid and waterfalls. However, it also contained a gate which only opened every few seconds. And therefore, it often happened that I crashed into a wall with full speed. I then either had to wait until the gate opened again or return to the main track. Over time, I became aware of the danger and always slowed down when approaching the gate.

It’s an absurd analogy. But it remembers me of the importance of slowing the f*ck down before approaching a goal. 

A lost sense.
The past few months were quite unique in my life. I worked full-time as what was supposed to be my dream job. Without a doubt, the tasks itself were great and interesting. Everything else, on the other hand, was quite different from what I expected. Yeah, I had this amazing job title – but I also had difficulties finding and identifying with the company’s values and completely lost the sense for myself.

My search for my role in the organization eventually ended up in a severe identity crisis – which led me as far as searching for the sense of humanity. I guess, I don’t have to tell anyone what this does with one’s happiness. One and a half months into the job, my motivation had vanished and my whole existence was based on discipline.

Still, I really wanted to give this amazing opportunity a chance – so I kept putting work in every day. Unfortunately, it just made me unhappier every day. Too many aspects were either missing or didn’t fit me. At this point, I knew I had to make a change. Still, deciding on leaving this once-in-a-lifetime-opportunity behind, wasn’t an easy choice. The only aspect that finally made me not extend my contract was me seeing how I was letting myself go more and more. Discipline also has its limits, I guess.

So, in order to  finally come back to my Lego Racers-analogy: Reflecting on those months somehow reminded me of how I used to crash into the wall with full-speed for weeks. I never managed to win the race until I realized that I had to slow the hell down and switch routes if I was about to crash. And now, the exact same thing happened to me in real-life. 

Rat-race.
Often times, choose to chase our “big goals” with all the energy we have – and at a certain point, we forget what’s in front of us. We chase the goal so hard that we neglect ourselves and let the goal, the job or whatever we’re chasing take over our personality. But eventually, a human doesn’t thrive on external goals. We all have an inner soul with unique values that will always exist – and we can’t and shouldn’t get rid of it. In fact, I think it’s one of the most beautiful aspects of humanity. 

So far, so good. Realization is the first step to becoming a happier person. But now comes the difficult part: Finding and embracing change if needed. In my case, I was in a quite lucky position: I’m now in the third year of my bachelor-degree and a full-time-position wouldn’t have been an option after the three months anyway. I still need money, but I can earn the little amount I need from basically anywhere. Lots of people are in a more difficult situation because they’re more dependent on a larger income.

Therefore, I can only tell you tell you what worked for me for getting out of my rut. But I feel like the approaches generally are quite similar. 

Finding alternatives.
The single most important thing which kept me alive during the last month definitely was finding and working on alternatives. I re-discovered coding and took digital marketing classes online – things that I once loved but increasingly neglected during my (blinded) path to becoming a business-journalist. I also visited more networking events and went on informal coffee-meetings than usual. Eventually, I found a small voluntary position in digital marketing which gave me enough hope to make a cut.

I’m not sure if this is the right path either. I just know that for now, I feel way happier focusing on my studies and doing this. I also found some interesting studying opportunities abroad which gave me new exciting outlooks. But at this point, it’s just speculating about the future. For the upcoming months, I’ll definitely be sceptical about setting precise goals I haven’t had a taste of yet. 

Making sense of it all.
Maybe, the past few months might just have been what I needed. On the surface, it was a professional journey, but behind the curtain it felt more like a sense-of-life- and personal-development-bootcamp. And from what I’ve observed, I’m by far not the only one experiencing such a personal identity crisis.

But why is this happening? Personally, I feel like it’s a generational phenomenon. Especially among young, hard-working university students. We’re supposed to chase a single big goal in the form of a job, power, influence or money. And we do everything in order to make it real. But doing so, we tend to lose sight of the big picture: life and everything around it. Instead of asking myself “What do I want to become?”, I now ask myself “What difference do I want to make on this planet?” and “How do I even want to live?”.

Big thinkers like Stephen Hawking or Steve Wozniak – he’s still my secret hero in computer business, sorry Steve Jobs – didn’t accomplish great things because of their job title. They just wanted to advance humanity and make this planet a nicer place for everyone. That’s what I feel is what life is about: Leaving marks and helping people better solve their problems. And it’s on us, what kind of marks these shall be.
