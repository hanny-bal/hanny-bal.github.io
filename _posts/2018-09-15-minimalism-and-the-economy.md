How will a minimalist lifestyle impact the economy? What if everyone would become a minimalist? This question hit me out of nowhere when I was enjoying a cup of coffee in my favourite coffeebar. 

Why? Because at first glance it seems like minimalism could completely ruin the economy we live in today. When nobody buys stuff, companies would run out of business. However, this is only part of the story. The economy would still keep existing, however, it would change significantly. 

## Principles of minimalism
To give the issue some context let's review what minimalism is about. Basically, it's about making more conscious decisions when going out to buy something. It's about asking the question "Do I really need this?" and restraining yourself from unnecessary consumption. 

So in this case, buying more consciously means owning less stuff. For the individual, this is a great way to make life simpler and find happiness and the things that matter. For the economy on the other hand, the outcome isn't quite as good.

## An economy based on consumerism
If everybody just stopped buying stuff the impact would be tremendous. Most industries nowadays are based on consumerism - buying goods in ever-increasing amounts. Fast fashion or packaged food for example. These industries would without a doubt suffer and a lot of jobs would be lost. 

Even industries oriented with a longer product-lifespan like the automobile industry would experience major cutbacks. People would use their cars longer.

On the other hand, however, our current economy everything else than perfect. It's not even rudimentary sustainable, horrible for the environment and the production of a lot of daily goods such as clothing often times violates human rights. 

Even more consumerism won't solve this problem for sure - or as Joshua Fields Millburn and Ryan Nicodemus put it on their blog: "Using consumerism to stimulate the economy is like fixing a cracked mirror with a hammer."

## Other investments
That's where minimalism can have a positive impact. It's not that minimalists don't spend any money at all. But they spend their money differently. Minimalists value experiences over possessions and rather invest in travelling, concerts, theatres or high-quality goods from local businesses. 

In a minimalist economy, these segments would grow. Traditional consumerist-based segments, on the other hand, would decline. Luckily, capitalism is flexible.

Due to the principle of supply and demand, companies had to rethink their strategy if everyone changed their consumption habits. If everyone said "hey let's don't buy food in plastic packaging anymore", retailers would have to adapt to that. They would stop offering those foods and instead turn to alternatives such as more high-quality local and fresh foods.

## Fundamental changes
Would it be possible to sustain the current size of the economy if everyone turned into a minimalist? Probably not. Even considering the shift industries and jobs. Overall consumption would be still be lower than today. 

Because of that, some countries would need to reinvent their economic strategy from their ground up. China is a good example for this. Even countries that rely on services such as Sweden would experience some amount of economic decline.

However, the reward for this change is big. A minimalist economy is about the long-term. It's about a better environment, more sustainability and more locality.

## It's happening right now
The scenario I described in the paragraphs above is arguably quite extreme. The chance that such a change happens within days or weeks is practically zero. It takes years or even decades to shift the economy. 

But looking at the global picture, this change is happening right now. Minimalism is a growing movement. People have started to think about their shopping decisions in the past few years. Local stores are experiencing a comeback and the travelling is at an all-time-high. We are part of the change.

The economy won't crash because of that. It will adapt. Like it always has.
