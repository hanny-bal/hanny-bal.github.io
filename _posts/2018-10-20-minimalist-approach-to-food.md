We all survive on food. This simple fact makes food one of the most impactful and controversial topics in today's society. The thing is: There's no single "right" solution to food. It depends on the goal. This could be maximising physical performance, cooking good as hell, feeding a family or minimising one's impact on the environment. 

With all these factors, putting food under the roof of minimalism is quite difficult. But not impossible. Here's how I approach it. 

## Why I rethought my eating habits
To start out, my nutrition never was unhealthy. It was pragmatic, quick and included lots of vegetables. Usually, I would buy several bags of frozen vegetables and eat it with noodles or lentils, beans or whatever I liked for dinner. For lunch, I would have some pre-packaged salad or a croissant and for breakfast, I'd just grab a cup of coffee to-go.

This was until a few week ago when I discovered Lauren Singer's TED Talk about a zero waste lifestyle (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pF72px2R3Hg). It made me look at my waste and realise that "holy sh*it I produce tons over tons packing waste". I haven't thought about my cooking habits for a while but at this point and I definitely wanted to change something.

So I rethought food from the ground up. How could I make my nutrition more "minimalist"? What I came up with, was a set of guidelines for myself. The goal: eat as healthy as possible, produce little waste and minimise my impact on the environment. That's how I approach it. So. What are these guidelines?

## 1. It's your food. So cook it yourself!
Cooking for yourself is could the most important point on this whole list. I've always done it and I still believe it's the key to good nutrition. When you cook for yourself, you have full control over what lands on your plate. It prevents you from buying unhealthy, expensive and often times wasteful packaged food and transforms food into an experience.

I was never aware of the last part until I stopped throwing frozen cubes of vegetables into a pan and called that cooking. When I put some actual physical work like cutting into my meals, I enjoy my meals much more. It feels more like a task, like a subtle art. The art of transforming raw ingredients into a full-blown, tasty meal.

Also, I don't really like going out to eat. Mainly because it's super expensive, not necessarily healthy and I'm way too pragmatic when it comes to food.

## 2. Keep it simple
As much as I love cooking I'm almost always busy and even if not, my priorities lie somewhere else. So my main principle always is simplicity. Basically - if you count "vegetables" as one ingredient - my meals mostly consist of two, maximum three ingredients. 

These are some of the meals I eat nearly daly:
- Fried noodle with vegetables
- Lentils with vegetables
- Apples with peanut butter
- Oatmeal with Banana
Salt, pepper and maybe cinnamon for taste and I'm done. 

I eat those exact meals over and over for years now. It's not very exciting, I know and it might seem boring and bland. But for me, it works. I like the taste and I don't have to spend any thoughts on what I will cook - because it's obvious. Also, I can easily pack those meals in a container for lunch.

Indeed, this style of eating is not for everyone. I know a lot of people who definitely like more variety in their diet. And that's great. Nevertheless I can recommend everyone to have some staple meals for busy times. It makes life much easier.

## 3. Unpackaged if possible
The main reason why I reflected on my eating habits was the waste aspect. I used to rely purely on frozen vegetables for my meals. I also always love a piece of dark chocolate or a protein bar which I bought in corresponding amounts. The problem I now see in those foods is the waste aspect. A bag of frozen vegetables leaves behind a plastic bag every time. The same accounts for a little bar of chocolate. Even yoghurt produces tons of waste if you add it up.

We all know the stories about plastic in the sea, micro plastic and air pollution. Of course, a single person can't save the world. But I think we have to start somewhere. If you add up the saved plastic trash from being more aware about waste it still makes a difference. And it shows people that it's possible and might encourage them to think about the topic.

When I go shopping now, I choose the foods with the least amount of packaging. Mostly fresh fruits and vegetables - which means this choice basically forces me to eat healthy. It doesn't work for everything though. Noodles, lentils or or soya drinks are still packaged in plastic and I can't do anything about that now. It's all about minimising my waste. I can't eliminate it completely at this point.

## 4. Plant-based for the earth
What connects food and minimalism for me as well is the environmental impact. Plants produce way less CO2 emissions than animal products (https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2018/may/31/avoiding-meat-and-dairy-is-single-biggest-way-to-reduce-your-impact-on-earth). Changing your eating habits is one the easiest ways to minimise your impact on the environment. So most of the time, I eat basic plant foods. Vegetables. Fruits. Legumes. Nuts.

I'm not saying you should give up everything you love in life for the environment. But please think about it. Without a doubt, we'll have to make some sacrifices for a better future. Not eating animal products is one of the easiest ones.

## 5. Watch your budget
Buying more consciously is great. However, what really determines our purchases in the end is our budget. Luckily, unpackaged plant foods are on the cheaper side of products. I'm a student my self and I don't have a big budget to spend on basically anything. So I'm always trying to cut costs.

Following my minimalist waste and environmental impact approach certainly made me buy less packaged foods. Especially pre-packed meals, chocolate bars and similar products are usually really expensive. I definitely was able to save some money in this regard.

*I got a little extensive in this article. So let's sum up what I'm saying. How do I approach food in a minimalist way?
- I cook mostly myself using lots of fresh vegetables and fruits.
- I have a set of staple meals I can cook without having to think about it.
- I stick to plant foods.
- And I avoid plastic packaging whenever possible.*

This allows me to cook healthy, good meals without spending too much thoughts on it. It also minimises my impact on the environment and on my wallet.*
